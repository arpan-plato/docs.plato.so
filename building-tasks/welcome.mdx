You're here because you're an expert at your job — and we want to find out whether an AI agent can do the parts of that job that happen at a computer.

That's the entire product. You build a realistic slice of your real work — the files, the emails, the systems, the messy details — and set the AI loose on it, exactly as if you'd handed the task to a new colleague. Then a grader checks whether the AI actually got it right.

You don't need to be technical. You need to know your field well enough to tell the difference between work that's done correctly and work that looks fine but is wrong. That judgment is the thing we can't get anywhere else, and it's what makes a good task.

## What a great task looks like

The best tasks share one quality: **a capable AI agent fails them for a reason a professional in your field would immediately recognize as a real mistake.**

- Not a trick. Not a hidden rule. A genuine error — the kind you'd catch in a junior colleague's work.
- Something that requires your expertise to even notice. If anyone could grade it, it's too shallow.
- Grounded in real work. The scenario, the documents, and the right answer should all be things you'd actually encounter.

If the agent breezes through your task every time, it isn't testing anything yet. If it fails for a silly reason (a broken file, a confusing instruction), that's not useful either. The sweet spot is in between: **hard for the right reason.**

## What you'll do here

<Steps>
  <Step title="Pick your area">
    Choose the occupation that matches your expertise from the list, and decide which real workflows are worth turning into tasks.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Build the world and the task">
    Set up the simulated workplace — the apps and data — and write the assignment the way a manager would actually send it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write the grader by hand">
    Decide exactly what "done correctly" means, and write the checks yourself. This is where your expertise lives.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Test it and make it harder">
    Run the AI against it, and keep raising the difficulty until it genuinely fails.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Submit for review">
    Send it to us, and we'll review and approve it.
  </Step>
</Steps>

The rest of these pages walk through each of those steps. Start with [How it works](/building-tasks/how-it-works).
