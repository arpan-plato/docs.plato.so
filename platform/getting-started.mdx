Plato is where you build a realistic slice of real work — the apps, the files, the data — and set an AI agent loose on a task inside it, then let a grader check whether the AI got it right. This guide is about **using the platform itself**: the screens, the buttons, and the flow from a blank world to a submitted task.

## Sign in

Go to [platform.plato.so](https://platform.plato.so) and sign in with the account we set up for you. You'll land on the **Worlds** dashboard — your home base.

## The Worlds dashboard

Everything starts from a **world**: a workspace where you combine **simulators** (the apps, like Gmail or Google Sheets) and **datasets** to build tasks. The same world can hold as many tasks as you need.

The dashboard lists all your worlds as cards. Each card shows the world's **name**, its **status** (for example *Running* or *Stopped*), its size and creation date, and a row of quick actions on the right: **tag**, **duplicate**, **share**, and **archive**.

### Finding your worlds

A few controls at the top help you navigate as your list grows:

- **Status tabs** — **All · In progress · In review · Rejected · Approved**. Every world moves through these as you build and submit it, so the tabs double as your progress tracker.
- **My worlds / All users** — toggle between just your worlds and everyone's.
- **Filter by name**, a **Tags** filter, a **sort** dropdown (e.g. *Newest first*), and **Show archived**.

### The top-bar tools

Three buttons at the top open platform-wide tools you'll use occasionally:

## Create a world

<Steps>
  <Step title="Choose the world type">
    Use the type dropdown (it defaults to **Scenario world**) at the top of the dashboard.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Name it">
    Type a short, descriptive name in the **New world name…** field. Use one clear name per world — you'll build a single task inside it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Click Create">
    Hit **+ Create**. The world spins up and opens in the editor, ready to build.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
**One task per world.** Keep each task in its own world. The world is what we review, so mixing several tasks together makes it messy to look at.
</Note>
